# Evidence review: municipal enquiry handling

Review date: November 7, 2026  
Status: Working analysis

## Purpose

Establish the published basis for investigating enquiry handling before defining the problem or choosing a solution.

## Sources reviewed

**S1. City of Saskatoon, Filling the Gap - A Vision for a Customer Experience Strategy, October 7, 2025.** Pages 1–2 describe the customer-service concern and the systems already being introduced. This is an administration report proposing further work; it does not establish that the proposed strategy was subsequently approved or completed.

[Read the report](https://pub-saskatoon.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=243825)

**S2. City of Saskatoon, 2025 Civic Satisfaction & Performance Survey, full results.** Pages 53–54 cover recent contact experiences and suggested improvements. These results are additional evidence, not the 2024 survey evidence cited in S1.

[Read the full survey results](https://www.saskatoon.ca/sites/default/files/documents/corporate-performance/communications/Engagement/2025%20Civic%20Satisfaction%20and%20Performance%20Survey%20Full%20Results.pdf)

## Findings and limits

| ID | Published finding | Interpretation and limit |
|---|---|---|
| E01 | S1, p. 2: 44% of respondents to the 2024 survey indicated that the City makes customer service a priority, a decline of 15 percentage points from the preceding year. | Supports a concern about residents' perceptions. Does not identify the process or department responsible. The underlying 2024 survey has not been independently reviewed here. |
| E02 | S1, p. 2: the first CRM phase launched in April 2025, covering six major departments and the Customer Care Centre. Further departments, a corporate knowledge base, and a resident self-service portal were planned. | The case must account for existing systems and improvements. This describes the position reported in October 2025, not a verified current implementation status. |
| E03 | S1, p. 2: interactions receive tracking numbers; information enquiries can be supported by the corporate knowledge base; service requests are assigned or dispatched to operational groups. | Provides a high-level process description. It does not establish staff's detailed assignment procedure, exceptions, or error rate. |
| E04 | S2, p. 53, Q15: 67% agreed they received good service overall; n=163, excluding “Not sure.” | Positive experience was reported by a majority. The remaining percentage must not be described as entirely dissatisfied because neutral responses are also possible. |
| E05 | S2, p. 53, Q15: 66% agreed staff resolved their issue or directed them to a department that could; n=162, excluding “Not sure.” | Combines resolution and referral in one question. It cannot be used as a routing-accuracy measure. |
| E06 | S2, p. 53, Q15: 63% agreed their request was responded to or resolved quickly; n=163, excluding “Not sure.” | Measures perception of timeliness, combining response and resolution. It does not measure elapsed time or locate a delay. |
| E07 | S2, p. 54, Q16: suggested improvements included faster response, follow-up and resolution (26%), easier access to staff or shorter phone waits (16%), and knowledgeable staff and correct referrals/information (14%); 36% said nothing. The stated base is n=66, excluding “Not sure.” | A small respondent base and grouped response categories limit interpretation. These percentages are not proportions of all City residents or all service requests. They are not operational failure rates. |

Q15 concerns respondents who contacted the City or one of its employees within the previous 12 months. The measures have different respondent bases. Do not combine their percentages into a single performance score or assume they describe the same incidents.

## Initial investigation question

**What changes to enquiry handling could help staff provide accurate referrals and timely responses?**

This is a proposed portfolio investigation question, not a request issued by the City. Referral accuracy is one possible area to investigate; the published evidence does not establish that incorrect routing causes delays.

## Questions requiring further evidence

| Question | Evidence a BA would seek | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| What happens from receipt to acceptance by a service team? | Staff walkthroughs, assignment procedures, and request histories. | Establishes the steps and exceptions before proposing changes. |
| How often are requests reassigned, and why? | Assignment history, reasons for transfers, and staff explanations. | Distinguishes incorrect assignment from legitimate referral or changes in the request. |
| Where does time accumulate? | Received, first-response, assignment, acceptance, and completion timestamps, with agreed definitions. | Separates intake delays from operational work and measures response separately from resolution. |
| What guidance do staff actually use? | Current knowledge articles, service ownership rules, update responsibilities, and observation of staff use. | Tests whether information is missing, unclear, outdated, or difficult to find. |
| What changed after the CRM launch? | Rollout records, process changes, and comparable measures before and after changes. | Avoids recommending work already completed and misattributing survey results. |
| Can existing procedures or systems meet the need? | Available system functions, configuration, staff training, and practical alternatives. | Establishes whether additional software or AI is warranted. |

These are evidence requests for planning. Internal municipal records and staff access have not been obtained.

## Effect on the portfolio

- Use the published concern as the context and retain source references for factual claims.
- Treat the earlier blocked-drain story, shared contact list, and handoff descriptions as exercise material, not evidence about Saskatoon.
- Use stakeholder-agent discussions to practise elicitation and explore assumptions; they cannot establish facts about the City's actual operations.
- Do not carry the earlier sponsor memo's concerns forward as verified findings. Revise that memo after reviewing this evidence review.
- Keep enquiry routing, FAQ assistance, and enquiry trend analysis open for assessment. No option has been selected on the strength of these sources.
- Establish baseline definitions before setting improvement targets. A prototype can test behaviour on synthetic examples; it cannot demonstrate actual municipal service improvement.
