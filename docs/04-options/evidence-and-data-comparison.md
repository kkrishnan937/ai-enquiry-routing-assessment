# Improvement options

Prepared November 14, 2026. Qualitative assessment; benefits and costs have not been measured.

## Decision

Retain guidance and intake improvements as a foundation. Select enquiry-type classification for the small experiment because its categories can be defined without resolving departmental ownership. This selection addresses test feasibility, not which option offers the greatest operational value.

## Readiness

| Option | Available evidence | Main gap | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Departmental routing | Starter enquiries and practice guidance | Agreed responsibility boundaries and reviewed destination labels | Not ready for a routing accuracy test. |
| FAQ assistance | Public service pages identified as possible references | Checked question-and-answer set and content owner | Further preparation needed. |
| Trend analysis | Synthetic enquiry examples | Consistent operational records over time | Real trends cannot be assessed. |
| Guidance and intake improvement | Draft guidance, approval records and tracking demonstration | Operational release, staff adoption and outcome evidence | Ready for document review only. |
| Enquiry-type classification | Defined categories, development examples and frozen evaluation | Independent domain validation and representative records | Synthetic experiment completed; operational use unsupported. |

## Source requirements

FAQ testing would require a source URL, page title, review date and supporting passage for each expected answer. Published pages can provide reference material but do not establish internal approval or a content owner. Unsupported questions must remain unanswered or be referred for review.

Departmental routing requires agreed ownership rules. Classification results cannot substitute for those rules. Trend analysis requires suitable dated records; invented examples cannot establish frequency or seasonality.

## Outcome

The selected experiment compared keyword rules with one local model. The model classified more clear examples correctly but handled fewer unclear or mixed reports correctly. Neither tested method met the basis for an operational recommendation.
