# Elicitation notes: referral responsibility and guidance

Date: November 8, 2026  
Participants: Business Analyst, Customer Service Representative, Customer Service Manager  
Method: Stakeholder-agent interviews, including verbal questions  
Status: Draft for BA review

## Purpose

Understand how staff handle unclear departmental responsibility, what evidence could identify such cases, and who resolves disagreements.

These notes capture stakeholder accounts within the portfolio case. Published evidence remains in the [evidence review](../01-plan/evidence-review.md).

## Representative account

- Staff consult service articles in the internal knowledge base for responsibility, required information, and exceptions. Earlier case decisions are stored separately in request histories.
- Some articles describe ordinary cases without resolving boundaries between services. The example discussed was dumped waste on a roadside verge.
- The representative checks the issue type, exact location, description, photographs where available, and current guidance. A familiar-looking enquiry is not automatically assigned using an earlier decision.
- If guidance is insufficient, the representative seeks supervisory advice and records it on the request. They retain responsibility for tracking the referral until a receiving team records acceptance.
- A case-specific agreement does not automatically update the knowledge base or establish a general responsibility rule.
- The representative flags a guidance gap using the request number, relevant article, and unanswered question. The supervisor raises it with department contacts. The representative does not always know how far that discussion has progressed.
- Repeated checks interrupt new work and create follow-up work. The representative has not measured the time involved or counted the frequency. They said these cases recur but do not represent most enquiries.
- Transfer history shows that a team changed; notes may explain why. Notes can be inconsistent or insufficient. A return note such as “Check location” requires clarification before its reason can be classified.

## Working distinctions

The BA summarised the following distinction and the representative confirmed it. Manager review is still outstanding.

| Category | Evidence needed |
|---|---|
| Missing information | Details needed to assess or act on the enquiry are absent. |
| Ownership dispute | Necessary details are available and the record establishes that teams disagree about responsibility. |
| Unclear reason | Available records do not establish why the request was transferred or returned. Further checking is needed. |

A return or delayed acceptance alone does not establish an ownership dispute. These are working categories, not approved reporting definitions. Their treatment of overlapping reasons still needs discussion before use in analysis.

## Manager account and clarification

The manager described the supervisor coordinating individual acceptance with receiving teams. Standing responsibility rules need agreement between the relevant service managers; Customer Service cannot unilaterally change another department's responsibilities.

The manager initially suggested escalation to a senior manager responsible for both services, then acknowledged that the role and delegated authority had not been confirmed. That route remains unresolved and must not be recorded as an established procedure.

## Interim direction stated by the manager

This is new direction proposed during the discussion, not evidence that the current process already operates consistently this way:

- Staff take disagreements to the Customer Service Supervisor.
- The representative continues tracking the enquiry until a receiving team accepts it. Escalation does not itself transfer responsibility for the request.
- The supervisor records the teams' reasons and brings unresolved cases to the Customer Service Manager for coordination with service managers.
- The request stays open while responsibility is disputed.
- Staff update the resident without promising an unagreed resolution date.

## Questions still open

- Who has final authority where the service managers cannot agree, and what delegation establishes it?
- Does the manager agree with the working distinction between missing information, ownership disputes, and unclear reasons?
- Who approves, publishes, and maintains changes to service articles after departmental agreement?
- How are guidance gaps tracked through to a decision and communicated back to staff?
- What coverage applies when the representative responsible for a pending request is absent?
- What follow-up and escalation times apply, and how are urgent matters handled?
- What request-history information is available for a review, and what access or information-handling restrictions apply?

## Analysis implications

The accounts suggest a possible gap between resolving an individual referral and publishing reusable departmental guidance. This is a hypothesis to investigate, not an established root cause.

There is not yet a measured baseline for disputed responsibility, related delay, or repeated staff effort. Reviewing a sample would require agreed definitions, attention to incomplete notes, and corroboration rather than counting every transfer as an error.

No solution or AI use case has been selected. A classifier cannot establish departmental authority where the organization has not agreed it.
